Dry, damaged hair — from heat styling, coloring, or just environmental exposure — needs a different approach than oily scalp does. The goal here isn't clarifying or controlling oil; it's replacing lost moisture and rebuilding what's structurally weakened in the hair shaft.

### What actually helps damaged hair

- **Hydrolyzed proteins** (hydrolyzed soy, pea, or keratin) — these are small enough to actually penetrate the hair shaft and temporarily fill gaps in a damaged cuticle, which is why "protein treatment" isn't just marketing language
- **Humectants** (glycerin, Sodium PCA) — draw moisture into the hair and help it stay there, which matters more for damaged hair since a compromised cuticle loses moisture faster
- **Natural oils** (argan, coconut) — mainly work as emollients, smoothing the hair's surface and adding shine, though they sit more on the outside than proteins do
- **Silicones** (like dimethiconol) — genuinely effective for smoothness and manageability, but can build up with frequent use, which is worth balancing with an occasional clarifying wash

### The honest tradeoff to know about

Most repair-focused shampoos still contain some form of sulfate cleanser, because you need *something* to clean hair — the difference is which sulfate and how it's balanced. A gentler sulfate paired with strong conditioning agents can still work well for damaged hair; a harsh, unbalanced sulfate with no conditioning support can undo whatever repair benefit the rest of the formula offers. This is why ingredient position (see our [ingredient label guide](/articles/2026-09-02-how-to-read-a-skincare-ingredient-label/)) matters just as much for hair care as it does for skincare.
