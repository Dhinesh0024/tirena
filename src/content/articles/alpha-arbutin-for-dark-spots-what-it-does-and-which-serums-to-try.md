Acne marks that linger for months, tan lines that never quite fade, patches of uneven tone: dark spots are one of the most common skin concerns, and also one of the slowest to change. Alpha arbutin shows up in almost every "dark spot serum" aisle, so it's worth understanding what it actually does before you buy one.

## What alpha arbutin is

Alpha arbutin is a skin-lightening ingredient related to hydroquinone, the long-established pigment-fading ingredient. Chemically it is a gentler, more stable cousin, which is why it is sold over the counter in serums rather than as a prescription-style treatment.

## How it works

Your skin makes pigment (melanin) using an enzyme called tyrosinase. Alpha arbutin interferes with that enzyme, so new pigment is produced more slowly. Here is the important part: it does not bleach existing pigment away. It slows the making of new pigment while your skin's normal turnover gradually replaces the darker cells. That is why results take patience.

## What the evidence says

The mechanism is well supported in lab studies. Human evidence is thinner: there are some small clinical studies, but alpha arbutin has been studied far less than hydroquinone or other pigment-fading options. A realistic expectation is a gradual, modest improvement in tone and in post-acne marks over a couple of months, not a dramatic change.

Two things matter more than the serum itself:

- **Sunscreen.** UV exposure triggers pigment production, so a serum without daily sunscreen is working against the sun. This is the single biggest factor in whether dark spots fade or return.
- **The type of dark spot.** Alpha arbutin is best suited to post-acne marks, tanning and mild uneven tone. Deep or hormonal pigmentation such as melasma is stubborn and often needs a dermatologist's input.

## How to use it

Most 2% serums are used once or twice a day on clean skin, before moisturiser, and sunscreen goes on in the morning. Start with once a day if your skin is sensitive. Give any product at least 8 to 12 weeks before judging it, and stop if you get lasting redness or stinging. Higher percentages are not clearly better, and 2% is the strength you will see most often in these products.
