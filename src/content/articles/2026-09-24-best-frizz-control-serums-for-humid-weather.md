Frizz isn't random — it's a specific, well-understood mechanism. Your hair's outer layer, the cuticle, is made of overlapping scales that lie flat when hair is smooth. In humid air, those scales lift, letting moisture into the hair's inner core. The core swells unevenly, disrupting the smooth surface that normally reflects light cleanly — that disruption is what you see as frizz.

### Why some serums make frizz worse, not better

Heavy, pure oils applied to already-dry hair tend to sit on the surface rather than seal it, and can actually trap moisture against the strand instead of blocking it out — leaving hair looking greasy without actually fixing the frizz underneath. Lighter, silicone-based or hybrid water-silicone formulas work differently: they create a flexible, breathable seal around the cuticle that blocks external humidity without weighing hair down, which is why formulation matters more than just "how much oil is in it."

### How to actually apply a frizz serum

- **Apply to damp hair, not dry** — this is the detail most people get wrong. A serum applied right after washing, while hair is still damp, seals in the right amount of moisture as it dries. The same serum on already-dry hair just sits on top.
- **Focus on mid-lengths and ends, not roots** — applying near the scalp tends to look greasy fast; the ends are where frizz and dryness are usually worst anyway.
- **A little goes further than you'd think** — start with a small amount and add more only if needed, especially on fine hair.
