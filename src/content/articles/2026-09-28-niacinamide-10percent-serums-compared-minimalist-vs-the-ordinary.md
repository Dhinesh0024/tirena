Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3 used in skincare for concerns such as visible oiliness, uneven-looking tone, and skin texture. A product label showing “10%” tells you the stated niacinamide concentration; it doesn’t prove the product will work better than a lower-strength formula or clear acne.

### What the 10% label tells you—and what it doesn’t

A small clinical study tested 2% niacinamide and found changes in some measures of facial oil, though the results differed between study groups and measures. That research did not test either serum here, so it cannot establish that 10% is more effective. A review of the clinical literature concluded that evidence for nicotinamide as an acne treatment remained unclear because the available studies were limited. ([2% sebum study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16766489/); [review of acne evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28220628/))

That distinction matters if your main concern is active breakouts. These are cosmetic serums marketed for oiliness, visible pores, texture, and tone; they should not be treated as proven acne medicines. Post-acne discoloration is also different from indented or raised scars, which a serum may not meaningfully change.

### How the formulas differ

Both products list 10% niacinamide and Zinc PCA, according to their manufacturers. The Ordinary lists a relatively focused supporting formula with Zinc PCA. Minimalist’s current product page describes a formula that also includes Zinc Glycinate, Matmarine (a marine ferment extract), and acetyl glucosamine.

Those additional ingredients make the formulas different, but they don’t establish that one is more effective. Minimalist’s claims about oil, pores, and tone are manufacturer claims, not independent head-to-head results. I found no comparative study showing that either product performs better.

Formulas can vary by market and change over time, so check the ingredient list on the product you receive.

### How to choose and introduce one

- **Choose by the whole formula, not the percentage alone.** The Ordinary may suit someone who prefers a more focused niacinamide-and-Zinc-PCA formula. Minimalist may interest someone looking for its additional supporting ingredients.
- **Start with one serum.** Using both together isn’t necessary and can make it harder to identify what caused irritation.
- **Patch test and follow the product directions.** The Ordinary advises patch testing, use on unbroken skin, and stopping and rinsing if irritation occurs. Minimalist’s directions say to apply 2–3 drops after cleansing and toning, then let it absorb. Follow the packaging for the version you buy.
- **Keep the rest of your routine comfortable.** Use moisturizer as needed and sunscreen during the day. Stop if irritation persists or a rash develops.
- **For persistent or painful acne, seek evidence-based care.** These serums should not replace established acne treatment or a clinician’s advice.
