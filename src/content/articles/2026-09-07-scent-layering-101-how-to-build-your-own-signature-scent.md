Perfume alone rarely lasts as long as you'd like — it evaporates off skin within a few hours. Scent layering fixes this by building fragrance into every step of your routine instead of relying on one spray to do all the work, and it's become one of the most searched personal care trends heading into this year for exactly that reason.

### How it actually works

Fragrance molecules cling to oils and moisture far better than they cling to dry skin. That's the entire mechanism behind scent layering: each product in your routine leaves a little more "grip" for the next one, so by the time you apply perfume, it has something to hold onto instead of evaporating within the hour.

### The order matters

1. **Scented body wash** — the foundation layer. Even a mild fragrance here means your skin starts building scent before you've applied anything else.
2. **Body lotion or cream** — apply while skin is slightly damp, right after showering. This is the layer that does the most work: lotion traps moisture, and moisture is what perfume molecules cling to later.
3. **Body oil (optional but effective)** — a thin layer over lotion adds another oil-based layer for fragrance to bond with, and gives skin a subtle sheen as a bonus.
4. **Perfume or body mist** — always last. Apply your actual fragrance on top of this base, focusing on pulse points (wrists, neck, behind ears) where body heat helps the scent diffuse throughout the day.

### The one rule that prevents this from going wrong

Stick to one scent family across all your products, or deliberately pick complementary notes. Layering unrelated scents tends to muddy both fragrances rather than enhance either. Vanilla is worth starting with if you're new to this — it's consistently the most-loved fragrance note in Indian body care, reads as warm and comforting rather than overpowering, and works well on most skin types.
