Sunscreen helps protect exposed skin from ultraviolet radiation, but the protection on the label depends on using the product as directed. SPF is a measure related to protection from UVB rays, which cause sunburn. A broad-spectrum label indicates protection against both UVA and UVB rays. In India, a PA rating is also used to indicate a product’s UVA protection level.

The sunscreen that works best for you is one you can apply generously and use consistently. Dermatologists recommend choosing broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher; water resistance is useful for swimming, sweating, or other outdoor activity. No sunscreen is waterproof, and water resistance lasts only for the time stated on the label. ([AAD sunscreen selection guidance](https://www.aad.org/spot-skin-cancer/learn-about-skin-cancer/prevent-skin-cancer/how-to-select-a-sunscreen); [AAD sunscreen FAQs](https://www.aad.org/media/stats-sunscreen))

## How to apply it

- **Apply before going outside.** Follow the product label for timing. The AAD recommends applying sunscreen about 15 minutes before outdoor exposure.
- **Use enough to cover exposed skin evenly.** The AAD says most adults need about 1 ounce to cover skin not protected by clothing; for the face, it recommends at least 1 teaspoon.
- **Cover the often-missed areas.** Include the ears, neck, tops of the feet, and any exposed scalp. Lip balm with SPF can protect the lips.
- **Reapply while outdoors.** The AAD advises reapplying about every two hours and immediately after swimming or sweating. Follow the product’s own directions as well.
- **Use other forms of sun protection too.** Shade, sunglasses, and sun-protective clothing add protection.

Sunscreen doesn’t prevent every effect of sun exposure, and using a high SPF isn’t a reason to apply less or stay outside longer. If sunscreen irritates your skin, stop using it and consider another formula or ask a dermatologist for guidance.
