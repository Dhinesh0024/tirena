Skin that feels tight after cleansing, looks dull, or shows fine lines more in the afternoon is often short on water. Hyaluronic acid is the ingredient most serums promise to fix it with. It does help, but the way it works also explains why some people use it and see nothing.

### What hyaluronic acid does

Hyaluronic acid is a humectant, meaning it attracts and holds water. Your skin already makes its own. Applied in a serum, it sits in the upper layers of skin and draws water in, which makes skin look and feel plumper and smoother. The key point is that it hydrates by holding water. It does not add oil, and it does not seal moisture in.

### What the evidence says

The strong evidence is for short-term hydration: skin feels softer, and fine lines caused by dryness look less obvious. The weaker claims are the bigger ones. Ideas about reaching deeper layers, filling wrinkles lastingly, or one blend of molecule sizes being dramatically better are mostly marketing or only thinly supported, and any visible effect fades when you stop using it. The percentage on the label isn't a quality score either. A 2% serum is common, but the overall formula and the rest of your routine matter at least as much.

### Dry skin vs dehydrated skin

Dry skin lacks oil. Dehydrated skin lacks water, and it can happen on oily skin too. Hyaluronic acid addresses water, so it suits oily and combination skin as well as dry. If your skin is dry, a serum alone won't be enough, and you will still need a moisturiser.

### How to use it

Apply it on damp skin, right after cleansing, then follow straight away with a moisturiser to hold the water in. Used on completely dry skin, it may feel less effective. You can use it morning and night, and sunscreen goes on in the morning. If a serum has extra actives, patch test first, since more ingredients means more chances of a reaction.
