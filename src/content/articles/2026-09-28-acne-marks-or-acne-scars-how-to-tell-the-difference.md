A spot can remain after a pimple has healed, but that doesn’t always mean you have an acne scar. The most useful first clue is whether the area is **flat or textured**.

Flat pink, red, brown, or darker spots are often post-acne marks. They’re changes in skin color, not necessarily permanent scars. A dip, pit, or raised area is more suggestive of a true scar, which involves a change in skin texture. A dermatologist can help tell them apart, especially if you’re unsure. [American Academy of Dermatology: Acne symptoms](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/really-acne/symptoms) · [Acne scars](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/scars?noads=)

### A simple way to tell the difference

- **Flat, discolored area:** More likely a post-acne mark. It may gradually fade, but this can take months or longer.
- **Indented or pitted area:** Could be an atrophic acne scar.
- **Raised, firm area:** Could be a raised acne scar.

Color alone can’t confirm what a spot is, and several types of marks can appear together. If you have persistent, painful, or worsening acne—or scars that concern you—a dermatologist can assess it and discuss suitable treatment. [AAD: Treating acne scars](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/scars/treatment)

### What skincare can—and can’t—do

A gentle routine may help improve the appearance of flat discoloration over time. Sunscreen is also useful: sun exposure can make some dark marks more noticeable or slower to fade. Treating active acne matters too, since new breakouts can leave new marks.

Serums may help with oiliness, blemishes, or uneven-looking tone, depending on their ingredients and your skin. They **won’t fill pitted scars or remove raised scars**. Those often need an assessment and treatment plan from a dermatologist. Avoid trying aggressive at-home peels or abrasive treatments; irritation can worsen discoloration. [AAD: Dark spots and skin of color](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/diy/skin-color)

### A cautious way to start

Patch test a new product and introduce one serum at a time, following its label. Keep the rest of your routine gentle, moisturize, and use sunscreen during the day. If a product causes persistent burning, swelling, or irritation, stop using it. If the marks are indented or raised, or you’re not sure what you’re seeing, ask a dermatologist before adding more serums.

**The takeaway:** A flat color change is often a post-acne mark; a lasting dip or raised area may be a scar. Skincare can support a routine for flat marks and active acne, but it won’t remodel scar texture.
