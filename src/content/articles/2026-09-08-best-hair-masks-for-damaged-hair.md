Keratin makes up roughly 85% of your hair's structure. When heat, chemical treatments, or environmental stress damage hair, the cuticle lifts and those internal keratin bonds break down — which is exactly what a good protein hair mask is designed to address, rather than just smoothing frizz on the surface.

### How protein masks actually work

Protein-based masks deposit hydrolyzed keratin or amino acids directly into the gaps left by broken bonds, temporarily reinforcing the hair shaft and smoothing the cuticle. Natural oils — argan, coconut, mafura — complement this by forming a lipid layer on top that locks in moisture and reduces friction between strands, which is why the best masks combine both rather than relying on protein or oil alone.

### Who actually needs one

If your main concern is dandruff, oily scalp, or buildup, a hair mask isn't the right tool — that's a scalp treatment problem, not a hair-shaft problem (see our [oily scalp guide](/articles/best-shampoos-for-oily-scalp/) instead). Hair masks are for dry, damaged, or frizzy hair that needs structural repair beyond what daily conditioner provides — especially relevant if you deal with hard water, high humidity, or frequent heat styling, all of which accelerate cuticle damage.

### How often to use one

Once or twice a week is the standard starting point. Overusing protein treatments can actually make hair feel stiff or brittle — protein buildup without enough moisture backing it up is a real thing, so alternate with a plain moisture-focused conditioner on the weeks you're not using the mask.
