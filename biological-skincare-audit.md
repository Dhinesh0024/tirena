## The Great Typology Lie

For decades, the industry has sold us the "Skin Type" model: Oily, Dry, or Combination. If you can believe the marketing, these are permanent identities. They aren't.

Biologically, what we call "Skin Type" is simply a surface-level observation of **sebum production** (the amount of natural oil your pores pump out). It's a metric of output, not a diagnosis of health. 

The frustration of "guessing" your skin type—wondering why a "dry skin" cream causes breakouts or an "oily skin" serum leaves you feeling tight—happens because surface observations mask the actual biological malfunctions. You aren't "Combination"; you likely have **Barrier Dysfunction**, **Cellular Signaling Failure**, or **Oxidative Stress** (essentially, cellular "rust" caused by pollution and UV rays).

When you treat a "type," you're guessing. When you treat a **biological function**, you're engineering.

## The Biological Priority Framework

Instead of asking "What is my skin type?", start asking "Which biological system is failing?" This shifts your routine from a guessing game to a targeted intervention.

### 1. The Barrier Audit (Water Retention)
**The Question:** Is my skin reacting to everything? Does it feel tight despite being oily?
**The Biological Focus:** The *Stratum Corneum* (the outermost "brick wall" of your skin). If the mortar (lipids) is cracked, water leaks out, and irritants leak in. This is **Dehydration**, not "Dry Skin."

### 2. The Signaling Audit (Regeneration)
**The Question:** Is my skin looking dull? Are fine lines appearing despite hydration?
**The Biological Focus:** Cellular Signaling. Your cells need specific molecular "instructions" to regenerate. When these signals weaken, the skin stops renewing itself efficiently.

### 3. The Architecture Audit (Structure)
**The Question:** Is there a loss of firmness or a change in the skin's "bounce"?
**The Biological Focus:** The Extracellular Matrix. This is the scaffolding of collagen and elastin. Structural degradation is a universal biological event; it doesn't care if you are "oily" or "dry."
