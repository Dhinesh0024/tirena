Most sunscreen marketing leans entirely on the SPF number, but SPF only measures protection against UVB rays — the ones that cause sunburn. It says nothing about UVA rays, which penetrate deeper, drive premature aging, and are the primary cause of the stubborn pigmentation and dullness common on Indian skin. That's what the PA rating is for, and it matters just as much as SPF.

### Reading a sunscreen label properly

- **SPF (UVB protection):** SPF 30 blocks about 97% of UVB rays, SPF 50 blocks about 98% — a smaller jump than the numbers suggest, but still worth choosing the higher one for daily margin
- **PA rating (UVA protection):** PA+ is minimal, PA++++ is the highest available. For Indian climate and pigmentation-prone skin, PA++++ is the realistic baseline, not a luxury upgrade
- **Broad-spectrum:** confirms both UVA and UVB are covered — always check for this label explicitly, since SPF alone doesn't guarantee it
- **Blue light protection:** an emerging area of research, not yet as established as UVA/UVB evidence — some studies suggest screen-emitted blue light may contribute to pigmentation, particularly in darker skin tones, though this is still a developing area rather than settled science

One rule worth remembering: SPF values don't add — layering an SPF 30 moisturizer under an SPF 20 foundation doesn't give you SPF 50, it gives you whatever your highest single product provides.
