Perfume price and longevity aren't as tightly linked as most people assume. What actually determines how long a fragrance lasts is its oil concentration, not its price tag — which is genuinely good news if you're building a fragrance collection on a budget.

### EDT vs EDP: the difference that actually matters

- **EDT (Eau de Toilette)** — 5-15% oil concentration, typically lasts 3-6 hours, best for daytime and casual wear
- **EDP (Eau de Parfum)** — 15-20% oil concentration, typically lasts 6-8 hours, holds up well through a full day or evening

All three picks below are EDPs, which is exactly why they can compete on longevity with pricier options — the concentration is doing the same job regardless of the brand name on the bottle.

### What to actually evaluate in a budget fragrance

- **Concentration listed on the bottle** — EDP or EDT should be stated; if it isn't, that's worth noting before buying
- **Note structure** — a well-built fragrance still moves through top, heart, and base notes even at a lower price; a flat, one-dimensional scent that smells the same from spray to fade is usually a sign of a cheaper formulation regardless of concentration
- **Sillage vs. longevity are different things** — a perfume can last a long time on your skin while barely being noticeable to others, or project strongly while fading fast. Neither is "better," it depends on whether you want a personal scent or one that fills a room
