Vitamin C is one of the most well-evidenced skincare actives available — it supports collagen production, provides antioxidant protection, and helps fade dark spots and uneven tone. But the percentage on the front of the bottle only tells part of the story.

### Why concentration isn't the whole picture

Dermatology research (the Duke Antioxidant Patent) points to **10–20% as the effective range** for pure vitamin C — below 10%, you're getting a weaker antioxidant effect; above 20%, irritation risk climbs without meaningful extra benefit. But concentration only matters if the formula is actually stable: vitamin C degrades quickly when exposed to air and light, and an unstable 15% serum can end up less effective than a well-stabilized 10% one by the time it reaches your skin.

### What actually affects stability

- **Form matters** — L-ascorbic acid is the most potent form but the least stable; derivatives and delivery systems (like liposomal encapsulation) trade a bit of raw potency for significantly better shelf stability and lower irritation
- **pH matters** — vitamin C is most stable and effective in a fairly acidic formula, which is also why some percentages sting more than others depending on how they're formulated
- **Packaging matters less to you, more to the brand** — dark glass or airless pump packaging protects the serum before it reaches you, but isn't something you can evaluate from the ingredient list alone
