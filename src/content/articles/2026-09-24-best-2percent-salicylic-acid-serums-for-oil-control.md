Most exfoliating acids only clean the surface. Salicylic acid is different — it's oil-soluble, which means it can actually dissolve into the sebum inside your pores and break down the mix of dead skin and oil that causes blackheads and clogged pores in the first place. That's the real reason it's considered the gold-standard BHA for oily, congestion-prone skin.

### How it actually works

Salicylic acid acts as a keratolytic, loosening the bonds between dead skin cells so they shed instead of building up inside the follicle. It's also chemically related to aspirin, which gives it genuine anti-inflammatory properties — useful for calming existing breakouts, not just preventing new ones.

### What it's good for, and what it isn't

Salicylic acid is well-suited to blackheads, whiteheads, clogged pores, and excess oil. For inflammatory or cystic acne specifically, benzoyl peroxide or a prescription retinoid tends to perform better in clinical comparisons — worth knowing so you're not expecting a 2% daily serum to do a stronger treatment's job.

### Daily use vs. a chemical peel — these are not the same thing

OTC concentrations of 0.5–2% are meant for regular, ongoing use. In-clinic peels use 20–30% for a single, more intensive treatment. These are two entirely different protocols with different risk profiles — don't judge a daily 2% serum by peel-strength expectations, and don't assume a peel is just "a stronger version of your serum" without a professional involved.

### How to actually start

Begin 2-3 times a week rather than daily, and always follow with SPF — salicylic acid increases sun sensitivity. Give it about 6 weeks before judging whether it's working; like most actives, results build gradually rather than appearing overnight.
