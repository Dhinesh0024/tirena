Both Minimalist and Pilgrim make well-reviewed peptide-based hair growth serums built around the same core actives — but they're not identical, and picking between them comes down to a few real differences worth understanding rather than just brand preference.

### Side by side


|  | Minimalist Hair Growth Actives (18%) | Pilgrim Advanced Hair Growth Serum |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Key actives** | Redensyl 3%, Anagain 3%, Procapil 3%, Capixyl 5% | Redensyl 3%, Anagain 4%, Baicapil |
| **Number of peptides** | 4 combined actives | 3 combined actives |
| **Approach** | Broader multi-mechanism blend | Narrower, more focused formula |
| **Best for** | Wanting several growth mechanisms working together | Starting simpler, easier to isolate what's working |


### What the difference actually means

Minimalist's formula adds Capixyl on top of the core three actives — Capixyl is generally included for its anti-inflammatory properties alongside follicle support, which can matter if scalp sensitivity or irritation is part of your concern. Pilgrim's formula is more concentrated in Anagain specifically (4% vs Minimalist's 3%), the active most associated with extending the hair growth phase and reducing shedding — worth prioritizing if shedding, rather than density, is your main concern.

Neither is a wrong choice. If you're newer to peptide serums and want to see how your scalp responds before committing to a more complex formula, Pilgrim's simpler blend makes it easier to judge. If you've already tolerated peptide actives well and want a broader approach, Minimalist's four-active formula covers more mechanisms at once.

### One evidence-based reminder

Whichever you choose, the single highest-evidence addition to either routine is free: 2-3 minutes of scalp massage after application. Peptide claims are still evolving evidence-wise; massage has directly measured results on hair thickness.
