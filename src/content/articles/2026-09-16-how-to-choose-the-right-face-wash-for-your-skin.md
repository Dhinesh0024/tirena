A cleanser's only real job is removing dirt, oil, and product buildup — but *how* it does that job varies enormously, and picking the wrong type for your skin can undo the good your moisturizer or serum is doing right after you rinse.

### The three main cleanser types

- **Hydrating, non-foaming cleansers** — clean without stripping, often built around ceramides and hyaluronic acid. Best for dry, sensitive, or barrier-compromised skin.
- **Gentle foaming cleansers** — a light lather that still removes oil and makeup without the tight, squeaky feeling harsher foaming agents cause. Works well for normal to combination skin.
- **Exfoliating cleansers** — combine cleansing with a chemical exfoliant like glycolic acid, targeting oily or acne-prone skin by addressing buildup inside pores, not just on the surface.

### The ingredients actually worth checking for

- **Ceramides** — genuinely well-evidenced for barrier support; clinical research consistently backs their role in lipid replenishment
- **Niacinamide** — similarly well-evidenced, shown to strengthen barrier proteins and help with oil regulation
- **Hyaluronic acid** — supports hydration retention, useful in a cleanser since even a gentle wash removes some surface moisture
- **Probiotics** — a newer, promising addition aimed at supporting the skin's microbiome, but the evidence here is still developing compared to ceramides and niacinamide — worth trying, not worth expecting dramatic results from yet
