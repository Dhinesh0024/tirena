For years, skincare culture pushed "more is better" — layering retinol, acids, and vitamin C into the same routine every single day. Skin cycling exists because that approach was quietly damaging people's skin barriers faster than it was improving anything. The method is simple: rotate your strongest actives across a few nights instead of using them all at once, giving skin time to actually recover between treatments.

### The 4-night cycle

**Night 1 — Exfoliation:** an AHA or BHA exfoliant to clear dead skin buildup and improve texture.

**Night 2 — Retinoid:** a retinol serum to support cell turnover and collagen production — the ingredient with the strongest long-term evidence behind it for fine lines, texture, and tone.

**Night 3 — Recovery:** nothing active. Just a barrier-repair balm or moisturizer, letting skin rest and rebuild after two nights of actives.

**Night 4 — Recovery:** repeat the recovery night, then start the cycle again from Night 1.

### Why the recovery nights matter as much as the actives

Skipping straight from exfoliant to retinol to exfoliant, night after night, is exactly the overuse pattern that damaged people's skin barriers in the first place. The two recovery nights aren't a break from "real" skincare — they're the step that lets the actives actually work without compounding irritation on top of irritation.
