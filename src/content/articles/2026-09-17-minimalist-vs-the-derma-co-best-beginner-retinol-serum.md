Retinol has a reputation for being harsh, and most of that reputation comes from one mistake: starting with too high a concentration. Dermatologists consistently point to **0.3% as the sweet spot** for first-time users — potent enough to work, low enough to avoid overwhelming skin that hasn't built tolerance yet. Both of today's picks sit at exactly that concentration, which makes this a genuinely fair comparison rather than one product being an obviously "stronger" choice.

### What the difference actually means

Minimalist's water-free formula is a real formulation choice, not just marketing — retinol degrades faster in water-based formulas, so a water-free base can mean better potency retention over the product's shelf life. The added Q10 is a mild antioxidant that supports skin alongside retinol, though it's a nice-to-have rather than a core reason to choose one over the other. The Derma Co's formula is more straightforward — just retinol at the same beginner-safe concentration, without extra additions.

Neither is meaningfully "stronger" — the real choice comes down to whether you want the water-free stability angle (Minimalist) or a simpler, no-frills formula (Derma Co).

### How to actually start using either one

- **Nighttime only** — retinol increases sun sensitivity, and breaks down in sunlight anyway
- **1-2 times a week to start** — resist the urge to use it nightly right away, even at 0.3%
- **Pea-sized amount** — more product doesn't mean faster results, just more irritation risk
- **Moisturizer is non-negotiable** — apply after, or mix a drop into your moisturizer if your skin is dry or sensitive
- **SPF the next morning, every time** — this isn't optional with retinol
- **Don't combine with AHA/BHA exfoliants or benzoyl peroxide** in the same routine — see our [skin cycling guide](/articles/skin-cycling-the-simple-method-replacing-daily-overload/) for how to rotate actives properly instead

Expect a "purge" period of increased breakouts or flaking in the first 4-6 weeks — that's normal, not a sign the product is wrong for you. Real results typically show up after 6-8 weeks of consistent use.
